# Progress

It's been a few weeks since I wrote my first blog post and released Fretboard Composer Pro to the world. I've now unloaded all the boxes in the garage of my wifes place in Mebane, and am slowly unpacking as we reorganize our two upstairs bedrooms into one bedroom and a work area.

Since the laset blog post, I've been in a loop of ignoring school, learning music notation, rewriting the composer in the app, and learning songs. I started a YouTube channel with my practice sessions which you can view [here](https://youtube.com/@kingparrac?si=8oo_adxVAckfEAZ3).

I'm still working on Your Call and Lover, You Should've Come Over, but it's not fully baked yet. I've decided to just play what I can to keep momentum and not trap myself with perfectionism.

One surprising thing is that most of my progress hasn't come from the advanced technique exercises that I built visualizations for in the app, or the advice that Maurice gave me about harmony, but from playing simple church songs, instead.

The first song I learned was "Hallelujah! What a Savior!" written by Philip P. Bliss. Jason, the church leader, arranged it for guitar as a series of simple open chords with vocals in the midrange. It was presented to me as a google doc with lyrics and chords with a recording and no music notation. The simplicity was perfect.

Because it was a simple chord progression with vocal intended to be sung along with by the whole congregation, I didn't feel embarrased about singing it, even though I've never sang before that. And, more importantly, the homeowner and great friend Shelly gave me a lot of encouragement when she heard it. This turned out to be a great on-ramp to incorporating vocals into my playing.

Since then, I've been singing a lot. Probalby the wrong way. My throat feels constricted when I try to reach high notes, and if I sing for too long my voice goes hoarse. One moment my voice is cear and bell-like and the next it sounds like an animal yelping. I've been very motivated to learn, not only because my songs have a huge vocal component, but also because I'm trying to femininze my voice in my transition to living as a woman.

Even though my vocals aren't ready for the stage, singing has been a great tool to train my ear. I'll play a note on the fretboard, and then try to sing an interval from that, and check it by playing the correct note on fretboard. This mirrors a few ear training exercises the Rick Beato reccomends in his book, but it's never really been natural for me until I had this context.

Sometimes I'm able to take a chord that I'm singing along with and make a short melody, or sing a chord tone and octave up. It's really encouraging to find that I have enough intuition to do these things despite never singing before, and sort of validates that what I learned on the guitar translates to general musical understanding instead of just note-avalanche-on-the-fretboard noodling.

Skye and I have a christmas performance coming up. I'm both excited and aprehensive about it. I'm the bands only lead guitar, so anything I play cuts through the mix. Last time I played in the church band, I'd written a complex melodic line that was perfectly matched to the song. I was going to show not only that I'm motivated, but that I can contribute something new to the group. I was going to be start basic, but try and work in some flair.

When service came around, I managed to play a small part of it in the practice. But during the actual performance I couldn't find a way to play it without trampling the vocals or respecting the timing. I ended up just strumming chords with lots of reverb, instead. At one point I got nervous and put the capo on the wrong fret... it took me a minute to realize what I'd done. Either no one noticed, or they were all so friendly that it didn't matter, anyways. Probably the latter.

Storied Church stands out from the rest of this town in a good way. It's the only queer friendly church in alamance county. I see it's relation to the community as a microcosm of the tensions around me. Mebane to me feels like a small place growing into something new, somewhere a little close-minded, where I'm not yet sure how to relate with new people.

I do have some fear. Not only are Skye and I two trans people in a red county, we're technically lesbians. I never thought that sentence would come from my mouth. But when I reach out with good will, I've been pleasntly surprised by how people react. Maybe I was the one with preconceptions, not the people around me. Or maybe it's both, and we just can't really see each other until we try.

I'm not sure what I'm reaching for, but I'm no longer pretending to be somebody else.
