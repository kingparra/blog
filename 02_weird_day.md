# I had a weird day

Yesterday I drove to Carrboro. I was going to have my first guitar lesson in years, play at open mic, and pick up a U-Haul truck to finish the move from my apartment.

When I opened the door, the first thing I noticed was the smell of wet cardboard. I carefully controlled how the door swung open to avoid bumping into the AC. Last we talked, the landlord told me he was struggling financially after building the stairwell to the upstairs units, and I felt guilty about asking him to replace the AC just a few days before I broke my lease. A stack of boxes leaned precariously to the side. Kinky Aloha, Toki Whisky, Bombay Sapphire, D’Adario -- repurposed boxes from liquor and music stores nearby now filled with tech, hygiene products, and clothes. Maybe that says something about me.

Without stepping inside I took a mental inventory: disassemble the bed, take the drawers out of the dresser, get a furniture blanket, wrap the mattress in plastic. I’d do it when I get back. I only had a few minutes before my first guitar lesson with Mauirce.

When I got to Maurice’s house, I was nervous. I never had a guitar lesson before, and I was determined to nail my songs before open mic tonight. I brought a video of Your Call and Lover You Should’ve Come Over on my laptop, paired with the app that I was using to learn standard music notation.

Maurice lived in an old white millhouse style building. He was a graduate from Berkley and had played for 25 years. He had a hand injury, but was still a good player. He led me inside through the living room to the hallway and into a small room filled with amps, boxes of sheet music, and stands with guitar tab on them. “It’s amazing that he can continue to make music with an injury, when people get good enough they can do it in their head... this is a person I can respect”, I thought. Maybe I was being insensitive by emphasizing the injury.

I brought my acoustic and laptop in. Things immediately took a left turn. “I can see you’re very motivated to learn these songs, but from my perspective, the journey of a guitarist is to develop their love of the instrument and technique, and songs will naturally fall from that.” I immediately thought-- isn’t that what I’ve been doing all these 12 years?

I explained my goals and we watched a video of Your Call. I told him I’d learned guitar from a book of scales and chords, without learning any songs. “That’s like learning English by reading the dictionary cover to cover”. I felt a bit guilty and nodded my head.

We finished the video. “That’s a pleasant sequence of arpeggios”, he said. I asked him to watch me play it and give some feedback. The guitar part was easy, but I struggled with the structure and vocals. At first, I was going too fast and ran out of breath. He suggested that I start out with a slower tempo, and I was able to get through most of the song after that. My voice cracked as I struggled to hit the high G4 that John sings when "do you remember, butterfly?” overlays the Em on the 7th fret.

He said he’d like to see a little of my own playing, to get to know me better. I played a series of power chords with open high b and e strings. “Would you like to know what this chord is named?”, he said, as I played an E5add9 on the 7th fret (part of a song I was writing). I said “Well, right now I’d like to focus on... wait, actually that would be interesting, how are chords named, anyways?”

“First, we have to talk about harmony. When you play a fret, there are actually multiple notes ringing out, embedded in each other. First there’s the essential, and then there are the partials. When you play a harmonic, like this <shows harmonic on g string 7th fret>, you aren’t shortening the length of the string, so how can the pitch change?”. We swirled around a nebula of topics... how a chord is composed of intervals. That the 3rd and 7th were special in some way I’ve already forgotten. What an inversion is (substituting a bass note with another note from the chord), what “first inversion” and “second inversion” mean. I asked him how he knew that power chord was an E5add9 instead of a B something-- if the B had partials of E and the E had partials of B, how do you know which one is dominant? He explained the idea of a tonal center and the exact intervals in the chord, how the intervals are named as you go up octaves. Core ideas of harmony. We talked about the harmonic series, and he showed me how to harmonize the G major scale with triads (something I just implemented as an exercise in my app but hadn't tried).

It felt like I was talking to a human version of my book of scales and chords. Except he could also make real music. Obviously, he was an expert. Before I knew it, we had only 20 minutes left. I told him about my efforts to learn music notation and he asked me “Why do you want to learn standard notation? That’s for classical. It doesn’t have any way to represent full bends, or microtones, or which fret to hit (there are five places on the neck that you can play any pitch)”. I know, I thought, I had just implemented a feature that would show you everywhere you can play a pitch when you click on a fret or note in the score. I told him, “Yeah, but what about timing: tab can’t represent note duration or time signature. I want to nail the timing for Buckley.” He loosely waved at a pile of tab printed out that had time signatures and note duration on them. It was basically standard notation with the note head replaced by fret numbers. “Oh...”, I said. He gave me a few copies of some tab for me to learn from. His own original work.

I left feeling validated that the things I learned were part of really understanding music, and frustrated that I wasn’t able to finish my songs. I went straight to open mic at Speakeasy. There were already a lot of people on the list. I waited a while and put my name down. I was second to last. There weren’t many people there. “Perfect, a low pressure environment”, I thought. Ten people played before me, and I watched every one. They were great. Definitely better than me, but I already knew that. I struck up a conversation with someone who had just played a Dylan song and we agreed to jam sometime ambiguously in the future.

I was up. Since I wasn’t ready to play Your Call or my Buckley song, I decided to play some of my original stuff. I played my series of glorified power chords... it was secretly the chorus to a song I’ve been writing about my friend. The EQ was bright and the bass notes were drowned out by the high e and b strings. Then I turned on my pedalboard, played an open G with lots of reverb, and froze the chord so it rang out infinitely in the background. And I played a solo based on G major and E minor pentatonic using the harmonizations he taught me. While I was playing, I realized that it wasn’t just a scale anymore. I was telling a story.

Surprisingly, people loved it. The people I knew at the bar and the few remaining performers clapped. The sound person, Jase, told me I was great. “This is not how I pictured things would go, but I’m happy that I got myself out there”, I thought.

I went to the smoking area to play guitar outside where I wouldn’t bother the patrons. It was cold outside. I was going to jam with someone, but they had to take off. Someone was asking Jase to use their phone. He was a man around my height in a dirty jacket. He looked around nervously.

Jase said she didn’t have one on her and she could check with the bar if he can use theirs. I offered to let them use my phone. “No, I want to use their phone”, he said while he gestured towards Jase who was already walking back into the bar.

Eventually, I started playing, and after a while the man (who I’ll refer to as “Friend” for now), asked to use my phone again. I let him. He placed a call that went straight to voicemail. Then he called again twice before handing me back the phone. It turns out, Friend was unhoused and taking shelter in the parking complex down the street. He looked scared, and asked several people if they had a place he could stay tonight.

Several people told him no. He pleaded with them. I remembered my empty apartment, how I stay up most of the night anyways, and had a pang of guilt. I saw raincloud starting to form, just barely visible in the night sky. It was freezing. I offered to let him stay the night at my place. He looked afraid and asked several other people if they’d help before accepting my offer. I said “are you OK, you look scared?” I decided to pick up the uhaul van tomorrow afternoon. 

Friend eventually hopped in my car and we went to my apartment. I made some small talk, asking him what he’s excited about in life, and where he could go if he had the resources to travel anywhere in the world. We were getting along. He made a few sex jokes in the car, and I told him I’d kick him out of the car if he mentions sex one more time.

We got to my place and I showed him around, which took less than a minute because the place is so small. I told him to feel free to use the bed and that he can close the door. We chatted in the living room and then he put his hand in his pants right in front of me. “What the fuck are you doing?” I asked. He said he was scratching his leg. I told him that if doesn't zip his pants up and stop mentioning sex ill throw him out even if it means kicking his ass. I mentioned that I was a rape survivor and that I was sensitive about the topic ans he was scaring me. I got my glock ready from the trunk of my car downstairs while he used the bathroom, put it in the center console, locked the car, and hoped I would never have to use it.

Eventually he calmed down. We started chatting about life. He asked me why I let him stay here. Then said he felt scared. I told him I wouldn't harm him and that he's welcome to stay, but he can leave if he wants. I'd even drive him anywhere in town. We talked about his situation. He was worried that his mother and sister had been abducted by a drug dealer. I asked a few questions and tried to empathize with what he was going through. He talked about his family with love. Mentioned his daughters hobbies. He went to the bedroom and closed the door. I worked on my IT projects and softly plated guitar in the kitchen for a few hours.

Around 3am he woke up “Stop it! Stop doing that!”. I asked, are you OK, what's going on? “When I think certain thoughts you keep making that screeching noise, and you need to stop! I know what you people are doing, I see weird stuff every day.”

Gulp. Had I just let someone with psychosis into my house? We talked a while, I asked him for examples of what he saw, and how he thought I was responding with my psychic powers. He talked more about his wife and daughter in Wisconsin. He asked me to rescue them with my powers, or by calling my ex military buddies. This poor person was in hell. He asked to make another call on my phone. Once, twice, then every 20 minutes. To lower the heat, I let him. Eventually I asked him to rest, and he went to bed with the door slightly cracked and the lights on.

I worked until 7am on my laptop. He didn't sleep. It was raining hard during the night and the wind was blowing. At least he's warm, I thought. After that, he pleaded to let me stay there and I had to tell him no. We went to Wendy's and got breakfast. I searched for a shelter but couldn't find one. We went back to the parking complex of the library where he was squatting so he could stay out of the rain. I gave him my favorite fuzzy blanket that I had in the car and cried. Eventually I left, feeling both heartbroken and a little proud that I had the sense of self security to help how I did, wondering what's next for this person in pain.

I went back to the apartment and slept for four hours, and now I'm at a cafe writing this and going through a list of missed calls. Life is strange, but people are beautiful.
